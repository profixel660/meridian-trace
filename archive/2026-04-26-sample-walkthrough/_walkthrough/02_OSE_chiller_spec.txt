2.4 GENERAL REQUIREMENTS
2.4.1 UNIT CASING

    a. Unit panels, structural elements and control boxes constructed of galvanised steel,
         mounted on a structural steel base.

    b. All unit surfaces to be protected against corrosion and UV resistant, suitable for given
         outdoor environment immediately after fabrications/erection.

    c. Factory mounted lifting rings shall be provided as part of frame construction.
         Components more than 25kg shall have permanent lifting eyes.

    d. Connections to all heat exchange equipment shall be arranged for cleaning access
         and tube withdrawal without excessive dismantling. Connections shall not pose any
         load on equipment.

    e. All hot surfaces must be protected/insulated.
    f. Units must have certified hook off points along the edge of the chillers for safe

         maintenance / removal of the fans.
2.4.2 EVAPORATOR

    a. Shells shall be hot rolled low carbon steel with seams precision electric welded and
         ground smooth in accordance to local standard.

    b. Tube bundles shall be unjointed seamless copper tube expanded into tube plates.
         Tubes shall finish flush with edge of tube sheet. Tubes shall be supported to prevent
         sagging. Tube plates shall be hot rolled carbon steel.

    c. Water boxes, air vent, drains and pipework shall be removable to permit tube cleaning
         and replacement.

    d. Drain connection to be provided at lowest point of water boxes.
    e. Vent connection provided and to extend clear of insulation.
    f. Thermometer and pressure drop test pockets to be provided as required for control

         and monitoring.
    g. Factory applied insulation suitable for outdoors shall be applied over cold surfaces

         below ambient temperature of liquid chiller components including evaporator shell,
         water boxes, flow meter and discharge / suction line.
    h. Insulation adhesive as recommended by insulation manufacturer and applied to 100%
         of contact surface including all seams and joints.
    i. Insulation materials to be UV stabilised, suitable for outdoor use.
    j. Fouling factor to be taken into account during selection.

    a. Coils shall be Copper & plate fin type aluminium.
    b. Provide coil corrosion coating suitable for environment which is rated to 6000 hours

    c. Tube and header material to be copper and adequately protected against corrosion.
    d. Internal baffles to be provided to prevent backflow of air through idle fans.
    e. All parts of condenser shall be free draining with no pockets in which condensation

    a. Due to site ambient conditions and/or to achieve a higher performance, an extended

2.4.4 FANS
    a. Provide low noise condenser fan selection to meet scheduled noise levels.
    b. Fans to be direct driven with EC motors.
    c. Fans to give isolation efficiency of not less than 95%.
    d. Provide fan guards.
    e. Fan guards to withstand under UV & corrosion protection apply adequate treatment
         per site location.

2.4.5 COMPRESSORS
    a. Provide semi hermetic two stage direct drive variable speed centrifugal type
         compressor.
    b. Provide suction and discharge isolation valves per compressor as standard.
    c. Compressor shall be able to reduce to 10% chiller design capacity and remain in stable
         operation.
    d. Starting amps of each compressor shall be 5-10 amps maximum.
    e. Bearings shall be oil free frictionless magnetic levitated bearings. They shall be
         equipped with automatic vibration reduction and balancing system. Bearings shall
         remain in operation during coast down including during power failure.
    f. Motor cooling shall be provided by liquid refrigerant injection system.
    g. Motor shall be a squirrel cage two pole, low slip high efficiency induction motor.
    h. A device shall be fitted to prevent pressure differential across the compressor causing
         backward rotations at a normal or emergency stop.
    i. Each compressor shall be capable of operating in the event a voltage sag of +/-10%
         in 3ms. Compressor firmware shall be capable of engaging compressor in the event of
         a voltage sag. i.e. non latching alarms. The vendor must configure their controls to

                                                                                                                                                              Page | 9
         ensure the unit will engage the compressors in the event of a voltage sag or any non-
         fast restart event.
    j. To ensure optimal operation of the chiller at lower ambient temperatures and or low
         load conditions, an appropriately sized heater or heating arrangement shall be
         included.
2.4.6 REFRIGERANT
    a. Unit to be shipped with operating charge of refrigerant.
    b. Offer alternate refrigerants which provide improved efficiency, capital cost savings or
         other benefits where available.
    c. Unit shall have electronic expansion valve, pressure regulating valve, factory mounted
         suction and discharge pressure gauges, liquid line sight glass and pressure relief
         system.
    d. Refrigerant circuit shall have load balancing/hot gas bypass for low-load operation.
    e. Unit shall come with refrigerant economiser capability to extend the performance
         envelope of chiller (subcooling). This is most beneficial in high lift conditions - low
         design chilled water and high design ambient temperatures - reducing work required
         by compressor. Vendor to specify whether this is included in base product or available
         as an additional option.

  2.4.13 FUNCTIONAL CONTROL
I. Mission Critical Functionality

       a. The chiller must avoid safety shutdown when operating outside design conditions.
            Chiller control and cooling load output should optimise to stay online and avoid safety
            limits being reached.

       b. The chiller must be able to accommodate intermittent loss of mains power without
            lockout.

       c. On loss of power during operation the chiller controller and its compressors will enter
            a fast restart mode. The fast restart mode must:

                 a. Capable of providing an alarm activation delay of between 30 to 60 seconds
                     (adjustable to site conditions). This is to ensure any low flow switches or non-
                     critical compressor alarms do not stop the chiller from fast restarting.

                 b. Be a robust and reliable system. The functionality will be factory witnessed.

                 c. The fast restart sequence must:

                           i. Restart pumps immediately (<2sec) and ramp at maximum rate back
                               to last known flow rate.

                           ii. Ramp compressors at maximum rate immediately after pump flow has
                               been established to achieve the setpoint of the unit. Controller should
                               know last known status and quantity of compressors operating prior to
                               power loss. Note: During a fast restart only the quantity of
                               compressors that were operating before the power loss should fast
                               restart. Once these are compressors are in normal / steady operation
                               the unit can engage the remaining compressors to achieve the
                               setpoint of the unit if necessary.

                          iii. Ramp condenser fans at maximum rate to achieve stability in the
                               system.

                          iv. Entire fast restart procedure should take <120 seconds.

       d. All external control inputs are to be written to the chiller controller register so that the
            current state remains within the chiller controller.

       e. In the event of loss of communications to the CMS, the chiller must enter standalone
            operation which uses last known states from the controls register.

II. Normal Operation
       a. If the chiller is selected into Automatic operation via the AOM switch the chiller will
            operate controller will receive the following write inputs from the CMS:

               i. On/Off Signal (LLI)

              ii. Change cooling water leaving temperature set point (HLI)

              iii. Cooling water flow rate setpoint (HLI)

             iv. Enable/disable water saving mode (HLI)

              v. Enable/disable acoustic limiting mode (HLI)

                                                                                                                                                                Page | 13
              Once enabled, the chiller will modulate all system components to achieve the cooling
              water leaving setpoint within +/- 0.5oC and the best COP.

         b. The unit must have the capability to limit the absorbed electrical power of the unit to
              align to the performance requirements outlined in Appendix A. The unit should not
              draw more power than required to meet its design conditions. Approval to disable this
              function must be obtained from the client.

         c. In the event of a non-critical fault or voltage sag the unit must have robust controls to
              ensure that it will attempt to restart its compressor(s) automatically & repeatedly until
              chiller starts or receives a stop command from CMS.

 III. Manual Operation
         a. If the chiller is manually enabled via the AOM switch then the chiller enters standalone
              mode. The chiller will start and modulate all system components to achieve the cooling
              water leaving setpoint within +/- 0.5oC.
         b. The chiller controller will use last known values from the chiller controller.

 IV. Water Flow Control
        a. The CMS will control the Pump VSD speed. Refer to AT-GLOBAL-TR-000015 Cooling
              Management System for more details.

  V. Adiabatic Systems
        a. If adiabatic systems are fitted, enable the systems by default at the manufacturer's
              recommended setpoint to minimise energy usage, however, ensure the adiabatic
              enable setpoint can be reset via HLI for optimised water usage.

 VI. Power Limiting
        a. The chiller will have a power limit register which will be set at the time of commissioning
              to the project chiller maximum power consumption as listed in the schedule.

VII. Water Saving Mode
        a. The default state for the water saving mode register should be `disabled'. In an event
              of site-wide water mains failure, the CMS will write an enabled signal to the register
              and the chiller enters water saving mode.
        b. In water saving mode the chiller disables the adiabatic systems and preferences
              energy consumption over water usage.
        c. The chiller shall continue to operate in this mode until a `disabled' signal is received.
              Once a disabled signal is received, the chiller returns to normal mode using adiabatic
              cooling to maximise COP.

VIII. Acoustic Limiting Mode
        a. The default state for the acoustic limiting mode register should be `disabled'. In an
              event of acoustic limiting mode is required, it should remain enabled by default, but
              can be disabled by the chiller itself, the CMS can only issue a command to disable
              Acoustic mode (In acoustic limiting mode the chiller reduces condenser fan speed to
              the project maximum fan speed to limit generate noise as agreed.
        b. If the chiller is unable to achieve the leaving water temperature setpoint during this
              mode, then the fans may modulate above the acoustic limited fan setpoint.
        c. Once a disabled signal is received, the chiller returns to normal mode by modulating
              fans to maximise COP.

2.4.16.2 Modbus Integration & Performance

All Modbus RTU devices provided are to be configured as Slaves, where the Modbus Master
is the CMS PLC or equivalent (by others). Each device shall meet the following requirements:

    a. No wireless communication interfaces or protocols
    b. Where the RS485 0V common ground reference is not provided on the terminal block,

         this should be internally connected to the common ground of the equipment
    c. RS485 End-Of-Line (EOL) termination available via DIP switch (or included termination

         module)
    d. Configurable RS485 communications settings as follows:

         · Baud Rate (default 19200)
         · Data Bits (default 8)
         · Parity (default None)
         · Stop Bits (default 1)
         · Flow Control (default None)
         · Modbus Address (range 1 to 247)
    e. Immune to permanent damage when exposed to the following conditions on the RS485
         bus, including:
         · Short circuit of the bus
         · Tolerance of up to 12VDC being applied to the bus
    f. The average Modbus poll response time shall be under 50ms. For example, if 20
         holding register (16-bit) addresses are mapped to the CMS, the successive polling of
         all 20 registers shall be completed within 1000ms (1 second).
    g. Where more than 20 holding register addresses are required to satisfy the specified
         required points, Read-Multiple Registers/Inputs/Coils must be supported with suitable
         consecutive register address layouts to ensure that a full poll cycle of all required points
         can be completed within 1000ms (1 second).
    h. All read-only points shall not be able to accept a Modbus write function command.
    i. All writable Modbus points (temperature setpoints, equipment overrides, etc.) shall be
         persistent and retain their values in the event of an onboard controller power cycle
         without requiring the Modbus Master to re-write to the point.
    j. Any writable points that can potentially be ignored/bypassed by the equipment (for
         example via local override) should be accompanied with a read-only "feedback" point
         that shows the actual controlled setpoint/command value.
    k. Writable Modbus points shall not have the potential to temporarily switch to read-only
         or reply with Modbus errors or rejections upon write function commands.
    l. All writable Modbus points shall take actual effect in the equipment within 5 seconds
         of the Modbus write command from the Modbus Master.
    m. There shall be a maximum latency of 2000ms (2 seconds) of any change of Boolean
         condition (On/Off/Trip/Fault, etc.) to being reflected in the associated Modbus point.
    n. There shall be a maximum latency of 5000ms (5 seconds) of any change of Analog
         condition (temperature, pressure, load, frequency, voltage, etc.) to being reflected in
         the associated Modbus point.
    o. Capable of providing a "Loss of Communications" status with field equipment (where
         applicable), for example, compressors, temperature regulators, and so forth.
