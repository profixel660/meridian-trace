5 EQUIPMENT AND SYSTEMS

5.1 CHILLERS

Select chillers on critical design criteria (Section 8.2) and peak thermal load calculation. The
BOD may nominate higher chilled water depending on project requirements. Align
equipment selections to AirTrunk standard OSE.

5.1.1 AIR COOLED CHILLERS

Refer to AirTrunk Air cooled chiller OSE specification for technical specifications and
interfaces.

Install on plinths located on ground or the building roof (refer to Architectural TR for plinths).
Minimum spacing between equipment to match manufacturer's requirements or greater to
suit project risks and CFD results. For large numbers of chillers consider the effects of
prevailing wind, building influences, generators and short circuiting between chillers.

CFD must be undertaken when large numbers of chillers are grouped on a roof at
manufacturer's minimum spacing. Refer to External CFD section.

Fix the base of the unit to the plinth on rubber vibration isolation mounts. For earthquake
prone areas, provide mountings in accordance with local codes.

5.1.2 WATER COOLED CHILLERS

Refer to AirTrunk water cooled chiller OSE specification for technical specifications and
interfaces.

Install chillers and pumps on plinths located in plantrooms. Minimum spacing between
equipment to match manufacturer's requirements for access and tube maintenance. The
chiller plantroom is to be passively ventilated to the extent that it can be considered external
to accommodate A2L HFO refrigerants.

Fix the base of the unit to the plinth on rubber vibration isolation mounts. For earthquake
prone areas, provide mountings in accordance with local codes.

Provide differential pressure transducers across all pumps and vessels. Provide universal
test points across all strainers, chiller vessels and pumps. Provide sufficient flow isolation
systems such that all components can be maintained.

5.2 HEAT-REJECTION UNITS (HRU)

Heat rejection units covers the range of options for different heat rejection technologies
AirTrunk deploy as OSE. This includes, open circuit cooling towers (OCT), closed circuit
towers (CCT), Hybrid Dry closed circuit Coolers (HDC). Heat rejection systems may be used
for standard water-cooled chiller systems, Elevated Temperature plant (ET) and liquid
cooling systems.

Select HRUs on critical design criteria (Section 8.2) and peak heat rejection load calculation
including all gains. The BOD may nominate higher water temperatures depending on project
requirements.

Refer to AirTrunk Heat Rejection Unit OSE specification for technical specifications and
interfaces for open OCT, CCT and HDC type cooling towers.

Provide any required local registration, labelling and certification as required by local
Authority Having Jurisdiction (AHJ).

5.2.1 HRU CONNECTIONS

Where basins require filling, filling systems operate via float switches and a solenoid.
Provide manual fast fill connections to each cooling tower. Provide dual water connections
from separate A & B hydraulic feeds at the common point of connection of the HRU

Each cooling tower is to be provided by bunded drainage system sized to accommodate any
surge and prevent overflow during a manual drain operation.

Each cooling tower fan module is to be provided with a dedicated VSD.

5.2.2 INSTALLATION

HRUs to be located on raised plinths or galvanised steelwork on rubber vibration isolation
mounts. For open circuit installations, ensure sufficient head to provide net positive suction
to the pumps.

HRU groups may consist of multiple tower cells connected with an internal or external
balance line.

HRUs to be installed in accordance with local AHJ. Provide condenser water treatment
systems to all basins.

5.2.3 CONDENSER WATER TREATMENT SYSTEMS

For central plant installations provide centralised condenser water treatment systems. The
systems must have redundant components to minimise downtime.

Condenser water treatment systems consist of:

    1. Side-stream filtration system (2N) with water drawn from the basin of each tower.
         Distribution system to ensure minimal dead zones within basin.

    2. Conductivity controlled salinity control and bleed system.
    3. Scale and corrosion inhibitor with measurement and dosing facilities

Where individual treatment systems have been nominated these systems can be N
configuration.

5.2.4 AIRBORNE CONTAMINANTS

Additional consideration in design of water treatment systems where air quality assessment
and risk report identify high levels of airborne contaminants such as dust from construction

5.12.2  GEORGE FISCHER HDPE

5.12.2.1 GENERAL
Process cooling water (chilled water, cooling water) or condenser water used for the critical
or non-critical systems may utilise George Fischer (GF) High Density Polyethylene (GF-
HDPE) as an alternative system to steel and copper systems.

Use of GF-HDPE must be engineered, installed and validated strictly in accordance with the
supplier's process and requirements.

5.12.2.2 PRODUCT
The nominated product is the GF ecoFIT (PE) system and shall have the following
characteristics:

1. Product lifespan 25 years at 20C operating temperature.
2. Pipe and fittings to be FM global approved
3. UV stabilised for outdoor use without solar protection

The coolFIT product is also approved for use where insulation is required on lower
temperature chilled water systems.

5.12.2.3 DESIGN
Sizing of pipework to be adjusted to suit the internal diameter of GF pipe. Reduced pipework
roughness must be considered in sizing of pumps.

Optimise the type of pipe joins based to minimise cost. Prefabrication to be driver in design
of GF system, maximising factory undertaken butt or infrared welding and minimising site
joins.

Provide spacing between fittings to allow for cut in for modification and repair.

Workshop drawings must utilise GF supplied Revit families for coordination. Workshop
drawings will be signed off by GF Engineering prior to the IFC issue.

Hangers and supports must follow the GF guidelines to minimise any deflection

Expansion loops or bellows must be design in accordance with GF's expansion guidelines.

AHU (FWU or CRAH) connections shall be branch saddle or socket wall fusion type welds

Vibration of plant and equipment shall be considered for final connections

5.12.2.4 CONSTRUCTION
Maximise offsite prefabrication.

GF to inspect regularly construction of prefabricated modules and installed pipework
systems on site.

8.9.4 PRIMARY PUMPING CONTROL

This control description applies to all critical data hall cooling water distribution systems.

When cooling blocks are sequenced to match load, the primary pumps operate as a parallel
pumping system on field pressure control. The AirTrunk nominated generic control logic is as
follows and must be adjusted to suit the project requirements. The system is controlled by
the Cooling Management System (CMS). Refer to the Cooing management system TR for
architecture and requirements.

CMS Flow Control Logic

    1. Acquire central plant load in the following sequence
         a. IT Load, by summating the relevant main PTU outputs, or if not available,
              individual IT load power meters and air handling system power meters in the
              space.
         b. Thermal Load, by summating physical Thermal Energy Meters connected to the
              CMS, or if not available, by polling the current cooling load from the operating
              chillers. The chillers have internal temperature sensors and a block flow meter
              from which the cooling load can be determined. Alternatively, a direct value from
              chiller HLI may be available.

    1. Calculate flow rate required for central plant load.
    2. Calculate the flow rate for each running cooling block and communicate to cooling

         block controller(s).
    3. Cooling block controller writes flow rate or flow rate offset to chiller HLI. The chiller

         then controls speed to primary pump to achieve the target flow rate as measured by
         the block magflow meter.
    4. Field pressure control:

              a. If the field pressure is <5kPa lower than setpoint and the bypass valves are
                   closed, the system flow setpoint is increased.

              b. If the field pressure is >5kPa above the setpoint and the master bypass valve
                   is >20% open, the system flow setpoint is decreased.

              c. Change of flow rate should be limited to rate of increase or decrease at no
                   more than 0.25%/s for the total system over 15s until the field pressure is at
                   setpoint.

    5. OSE chillers can generally run at a minimum flow rate of 40-50% design flow. A
         project optimised staging table will be included in schematic design.

    6. The bypass system:

              a. Bypass valves must not be located in the same fault domain but should be
                   located near each other within the index ring loop in the far field.

              b. shall control to a field differential pressure setpoint of approximately
                   150kPaas measured by the lowest field differential pressure sensors (low
                   select). The setpoint will be adjusted during commissioning.

              c. To have an parallel control loop with the variable primary pumping control
                   system to relieve pressure (Sys DP sp + 15 kPa typ.).

              d. Multiple bypasses should operate sequentially in a master/slave arrangement
                   to form one bypass control system, not in parallel

              e. In the event that no chillers are running (power failure) the master bypass
                   valve will open to 100% until a running status has been received on a chiller.
                   Once a chiller is running for 2 minutes, the bypass valve will return to normal
                   operation.

         a. must be selected for the correct range of operation.
         b. must configured in two groups of two sensors (4 total) along the length of the

    1. There must be a minimum of 1 chiller running at all times.

8.11 WATER COOLED CHILLER SYSTEM

The AirTrunk standard OSE water cooled chiller comprises of a chiller system made up of
oil-free, magnetic bearing centrifugal compressors, a single refrigerant circuit with a water-
based condenser and evaporator.

The chillers are a fully standalone unit with controls designed to deliver constant temperature
water +/-0.2C around setpoint. The chillers are designed with a fast-restart feature.

Each chiller is served by a primary cooling water pump circulating water from the process
cooling loop and a condenser water pump circulating water through the cooling towers.
Magnetic flow meters are to be provided on both the cooling water and condenser water side
for water flow control logic.

BOD.

The chillers, pumps and tower form a cooling block that acts as a single redundant block
complete with power and block controller.

8.12 ELEVATED TEMPERATURE COOLING (ET) SYSTEM

The AirTrunk standard ET (compressorless) central plant is comprised of cooling blocks
made up by closed circuit OSE Heat Rejection Units (HRUs) in conjunction with primary
pumps & flow meters. In operation, the ET plant can generate cold enough leaving water to
satisfy the data hall indoor SLA temperature conditions.

The OSE HRUs typically have no integrated controls and therefore rely on the CMS block
controllers for operational function & control. The method of flow control and staging of plant
is the same as air-cooled & water-cooled chiller systems.

Each HRU is served by a primary cooling water pump circulating water from the process
cooling loop.

The pump, valve train and HRU form a redundant block complete with power system and
CMS block controller. Each block will form part of staging sequence.

8.12.3.3 HRU TEMPERATURE CONTROL
Temperature control of the cooling water is achieved by varying the fan speed of the
operating HRUs.

The HRU leaving water setpoint is generally set lower than the design peak wet-bulb
temperature would achieve. This is to provide some reduction exposure of high space
temperature for the IT equipment.

The HR leaving water temperature may temporarily increase up to the design maximum
temperature during periods of high ambient wet-bulb temperature and if the fan speed of the
is at or close to maximum. The setpoint temperature will be determined in the BOD and align
to the project PUE targets.

8.12.3.4 HYBRID DRY COOLER (HDC) EVAPORATIVE SYSTEM CONTROL
Where HDCs are used, they have two operating modes, controlled by the CMS:

    1. Dry Mode: No evaporative spray required to meet the cooling demand. Fanspeed
         <90%, generally corresponding to low ambient temperatures. The N load crossover
         temperature will be determined on a project selection and correspond with a WUE
         target.

    2. Wet Mode: Evaporative spray active. This is required when the ambient conditions
         and heat rejection load is high enough that the unit can no longer meet the cooling
         demand without the evaporative cooling component. This mode is generally activated
         as the unit fanspeed exceeds 90% in Dry Mode.
